## Content rules for the Spanish bank (A1 and the model for 046)
Columns as German: `word, article, part_of_speech, ipa, meaning_en, meaning_it, example, example_it, cultural_note, grammar_angle, topic_domain, session_scenario, example_en, cultural_note_en`.
- **Word form:** nouns without article in `word`, `article` = `el`/`la` (the article that goes with the singular; a noun that is `el` with a feminine meaning-word like *el agua* keeps `el`; nouns with both genders by meaning (*el/la estudiante*) use the more common form and say so in the cultural note). Verbs in the infinitive; adjectives in the masculine singular; `article` is `-` for non-nouns.
- **Variety:** Latin American neutral. No `vosotros` forms and no Spain-only words (`vale`, `coche`, `ordenador`). Choose the word with the widest reach; when a word is clearly regional, say so in `cultural_note` ("En España se dice …").
- **IPA:** Latin American pronunciation, one transcription, no slashes (as in the German bank).
- **Example sentence:** 4–10 words, natural, uses the word itself in the form it has in the bank (nouns: the singular; verbs: a conjugated or infinitive form that **contains the infinitive stem**; the validator checks that the word or its stem appears, see `exampleRules`).
- **Topics** (the same 8 as German): food, travel, work, culture, home, leisure, health, shopping; roughly even, none under 8 %.
- **Parts of speech:** nouns ≈ 45 %, verbs ≈ 25 %, adjectives ≈ 12 %, the rest function words and adverbs (A1 needs *yo, tú, usted, aquí, muy, también, …*).
- **Grammar tags:** every entry has one code from the A1 set (`NOUN_GENDER, ADJ_AGREEMENT, VERB_PRESENT_REGULAR, SER_ESTAR, NEGATION_NO`) **or** a code of a later level only when the sentence really exercises it (not at A1: leave to 046). Nouns → `NOUN_GENDER` (the drill uses the article), adjectives → `ADJ_AGREEMENT`, regular verbs → `VERB_PRESENT_REGULAR`.
- **Choice-tagged sentences (`SER_ESTAR`):** at most **20 entries** at A1 (≈7 %), every one a sentence where *only one* of ser/estar is correct and the verb form is one of the pairs of `pack.choices` (never `fue`). E.g. *Mi madre está cansada.* (estar: state), *Ella es doctora.* (ser: profession), *El libro está en la mesa.* (estar: location). Forbidden: sentences where both are defensible (*Es/Está aburrido*, *es/está nervioso*). The `word` of such an entry is the adjective/noun the sentence is about. (`POR_PARA` and `DIRECT_OBJECT_PRONOUN` start at A2.)
- **Italian meanings and notes:** `meaning_it`, `example_it`, `cultural_note` are natural Italian; the cultural note is one or two sentences, true, specific (a custom, a usage difference, a false friend with Italian: *burro* = asino, *salir* ≠ salire, *embarazada* ≠ imbarazzata), not filler. English columns mirror them. False friends with Italian are welcome where they teach something real.
- **No duplicates** within the bank; no invented words; no offensive vocabulary at A1.
- **Generation:** the Developer drafts the 300 rows in batches (~50 per batch, one topic mix per batch), then runs the validator and fixes what it finds. Machine drafting is accepted; the human review is TASK-046.

## Acceptance Criteria
- [ ] With `"draft": true`: `build:wordbank` writes `src/data/wordbank/es/A1.json` from `A1.tsv` and only warns (`es: no A2.tsv (draft)`) for missing levels; `validate-wordbank` validates the A1 JSON and ignores absent levels; `set-active-language es` still refuses.
- [ ] A language without `draft` and with a missing level file still fails the build/validation (regression test with a fixture).
- [ ] `es/A1.tsv` has 280–320 rows, 14 columns, no duplicates, every topic ≥ 8 %, nouns 40–50 %, ≤ 20 `SER_ESTAR` rows.
- [ ] Validator `exampleRules`: the example contains the word (case-insensitive, accents kept, whole word) **or**, for verbs, a token that starts with the word's stem (`stemLength` letters of the infinitive minus `ar/er/ir`, minimum 3 letters); 4–10 tokens; ends with `.` `?` or `!`; starts with an upper-case letter; no `vosotros|vale\b|coche\b|ordenador\b` (the `forbidden` regex list in `language.json`); `¿` and `?` appear together.
- [ ] Every `SER_ESTAR` entry contains a pack pair member and the validator's `ruleChecks` pass (TASK-044); a test additionally checks that no such sentence contains **both** members of any pair.
- [ ] The A1 JSON works with the pack: for every entry the article question is built when `part_of_speech = noun`; at least 100 of the entries yield a typed-cloze, a reorder or a find-the-mistake model (property test with `setActivePackForTests(es)` and the A1 JSON loaded through the existing builders); every choice question has its answer in its options.
- [ ] `tsc` clean; `npm test` green (German unchanged); `CONTEXT.md` updated (A1 done, 046 next).

`language.json` additions:
```json
"exampleRules": { "minTokens": 4, "maxTokens": 10, "stemMinLength": 3,
                  "forbidden": ["\\bvosotros\\b", "\\bvale\\b", "\\bcoche\\b", "\\bordenador\\b"] }
```
Validator messages: `…: example must contain the word or its stem`, `…: example has N tokens (4–10)`, `…: example must start upper-case and end with . ? or !`, `…: example uses forbidden form /<re>/`, `…: "¿" and "?" must come together`. Rules apply only when `exampleRules` is present (German has none, so its bank is unchanged and not re-validated more strictly).
